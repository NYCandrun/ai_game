# Panel review 6: management-consulting / corporate-strategy reading

Reviewer frame: ex-strategy-firm MBA, now corporate strategy at a fintech, reading "Aladdin Studio, learned with AI" (18 pp) as a work sample for a VP Technical Product Marketing role. Judged on the firm's standard tests: governing thought, pyramid, MECE, "so what" exhibits, numbers, fact/inference/recommendation hygiene, prioritised recommendations, risks, hypothesis testing, and presentability.

## Verdict

This is an unusually well-evidenced research brief wearing the clothes of a recommendation. The sourcing discipline (graded register, timestamps, disclosed corrections, "author's tally" caveats) is better than most consulting output, and four of the findings are genuinely new to a reader who already knows the product: Studio has never been said on an earnings call in six years, seven rivals publish an AI connector and Aladdin does not, a client's engineer is committing to the SDK while its 10-K records the migration, and a buyer-side partner now asks "what's the API suite" in RFPs. But the document is organised inductively (six pages of "what Studio is" before the first finding), the governing thought is not on page one in one sentence, the title and the three headline tiles describe the method rather than the answer, 14 of 18 exhibits carry descriptive rather than action titles, the single most important number (17x API-call growth) has no chart, nothing is sized, the six "quick wins" are unprioritised and unsequenced, and the risks that would overturn the recommendations are present as facts in the body (the 10-K pricing terms, the Access.Internal tag) but never converted into a "what would change my view" section. A partner would say: the answer is in here, move it to page one and put a number on it. As a work sample: A- for evidence, B- for argument architecture, B+ overall. Would advance; would not go to a client without one restructuring pass.

## Findings

### A. Governing thought and pyramid

1. **p1, p2. The title and the opening sentence describe the method, not the answer.** Current: "Aladdin Studio, learned with AI" and "An outsider with AI agents and about a day can assemble a detailed, sourced picture of Aladdin Studio from BlackRock's own specifications, pages and recordings, plus filings and code. This is what it looks like, what it says about direction, and the quick wins for marketing Studio." Why it matters: a reader with five minutes gets the table of contents, not the conclusion; the conclusion is on the same page but two paragraphs down and split across three. Change: open with a one-sentence governing thought and make the subtitle carry it, for example: "Studio is the vehicle for the growth BlackRock shows investors, the customer proof and the buyer demand already exist in public, and none of it carries Studio's name; six moves fix that without opening the gate." Everything else on p2 then supports that sentence.

2. **p2. The three headline tiles are inputs, not outputs.** Current: "54 Public API specifications read", "128 Recordings transcribed and checked", "17x Projected growth in API calls". Why: two of the three are effort metrics ("look how much was read"); only 17x is a business number. In a consulting review the tiles are the three facts that carry the argument. Change: 17x (API calls by 2030, vs 4x the next measure); 0 of 26 (earnings calls that say "Studio"); 7 vs 0 (providers with a public AI connector vs Aladdin). Move 54 and 128 to the method box.

3. **pp3–8. The structure is inductive: six pages of description before the first finding.** The head of GTM knows what Studio is; Part 1 is 40 percent of the body and its value to this reader is "what the outside sees", not "what Studio is". Why: pyramid order is answer, findings, evidence; here it is evidence, findings, answer. Change: after the summary, go straight to the four findings and the wins; cut Part 1 to a two-page "The outside view" (Ex 3, Ex 5, Ex 7, merged Ex 8/9) and move Ex 1, 2, 4, 6 to an appendix.

4. **p9. Part 2 has no part header.** Part 1 (p3) and Part 3 (p14) carry a "Part N" label and title; Part 2 begins at "1. Public customer proof already exists" with no "Part 2: Four findings" marker. Why: the visible skeleton of the pyramid is broken exactly where the argument starts. Change: add the Part 2 label and, in one line under it, the four finding headlines so the reader sees the whole second level at once.

### B. MECE and logic

5. **p10, Finding 2 bundles two arguments and the second is orphaned.** The headline is "API consumption and the whole portfolio", and the second paragraph (eFront, Preqin, "over 10 different transaction types", Azure hosting, AWS second option) is a separate story about private markets and hosting. Why: no quick win in Ex 17 draws on it, so it is evidence without a conclusion. Change: either split it into its own finding with a win attached (a private-markets-through-Data-Cloud story is a natural Studio proof point) or cut it to one sentence and keep Finding 2 about API consumption.

6. **p9. An assumption is written as a fact.** Current: "in custom-application land, proof from one firm counts even for a dissimilar buyer." Why: it is the load-bearing premise of QW1 (named proof from public buyers serving insurers and asset managers) and nothing in the register supports it. Change: label it as an assumption and name the test (a sales-team interview, or the Ex 18 question on what stops clients being named).

7. **p10. The document contains the fact that undercuts QW2 and does not confront it.** Current: "BlackRock's 10-K says technology services fees are generally set by the value of positions on the platform or at a fixed rate, and can depend on subscriptions, go-lives and support; API calls appear in none of those terms." Why: if API calls are not monetised, investor relations has no reason to name Studio beside the metric, and QW2 ("Studio's name on the API growth story") collapses to a client-deck win. Change: carry this into a risk line against QW2 and reframe the win as client-facing first, investor-facing only if IR chooses to show API calls again.

### C. Exhibits: "so what" and action titles

8. **Only four of 18 exhibits have action titles (Ex 1, 3, 14 and, hedged, 7).** The rest are labels. Examples and proposed rewrites: Ex 5 (p6) "The public catalog: 54 specifications across 11 domains" becomes "Nearly half the public catalog is alpha, and investment operations is the largest domain"; Ex 10 (p9) "JPMorgan Chase payments to BlackRock for Aladdin" becomes "JPMorgan's Aladdin payments more than tripled in two years"; Ex 12 (p10) "Mentions of Studio terms on 26 BlackRock earnings calls" becomes "Studio has never been said on an earnings call in six years"; Ex 16 (p13) "What attendees of BlackRock's Build vs. Buy webinar said" becomes "Half of attendees would hand compliance reporting to a vendor; AI and data demand is the top driver"; Ex 17 (p14) "Quick wins" becomes "Six no-gate moves, three deliverable this quarter". Why: a consulting reader reads titles only on the first pass; descriptive titles make the first pass empty.

9. **p10. The key number has no exhibit.** Current, in body text: "API calls across BlackRock and Aladdin would grow 17 times by 2030, against four times for model portfolios, two times for portfolios and 1.3 times for trade allocations." Why: this is the one chart the head of GTM would take to investor relations and it exists only as a sentence. Change: a four-bar exhibit (17x, 4x, 2x, 1.3x) with the action title "API calls are the fastest-growing measure BlackRock showed investors, by four times", and a footnote that no base was given.

10. **p7–8, Ex 7 is a benchmark the author disowns.** Current: "The exhibit shows each vendor's model, not a score against Aladdin" and "Bloomberg, Clearwater and MSCI may be underrated: some pages blocked automated access." Why: the exhibit sums eight unweighted criteria into a total, highlights Studio's row, then tells the reader not to read it as a score; and three of eleven rows carry admitted measurement error. A consultant either owns the benchmark or does not show the total. Change: re-check the three blocked platforms by hand, add Charles River / State Street Alpha and SS&C (the closest front-to-back rivals, absent here), keep the total, and write the so-what as the title: "Studio is the only investment platform with an open SDK and trails every data platform on the other seven criteria."

11. **p9, Ex 10 chart hygiene.** The legend lists "Payments for Aladdin" and "Integration services" but only one series is visible; 2016 is an empty category; 2018 to 2022 bars are unlabelled while the text gives only the 2017 and 2023 endpoints. Why: a reviewer will ask whether the intermediate years were pulled; an empty legend entry reads as a chart built from a template. Change: label every year or show the four years quoted and say so; drop the second series unless it has data; add the custody caveat to the title, not just the footnote.

12. **pp4–8. Redundant and low-yield exhibits.** Ex 8 and Ex 9 (p8) present the same five speakers twice; Ex 3 and Ex 4 (p5) list the same components twice; Ex 2 (p4) is a dense product walkthrough whose only marketing-relevant point ("the specifications hold no position or event API") is in the footnote. Why: 18 exhibits for a 20-minute conversation is one per minute, and these six occupy a third of the page budget. Change: merge 8 and 9 into one speaker-by-pillar matrix; merge 3 and 4; move Ex 2 to the appendix with its footnote promoted to a sentence in Part 1.

13. **p9, Ex 11 is missing the number that sits in its own sources.** Current footnote: "Weights and outcomes are from an earlier draft of this research (October 2026) and its reading of the notices, not re-pulled." Why: UK Find a Tender award notices and USAspending records carry contract values; three public contract values would make the named-proof programme concrete and would be the only revenue numbers in the document apart from JPMorgan. "Not re-pulled" also reads as unfinished work in a client-facing piece. Change: re-pull the three notices, add a "Contract value" column, and delete the footnote.

### D. Numbers and sizing

14. **No sizing anywhere; p14 defers it.** Current: "The answers would confirm or correct the findings before any of the wins is sized." Why: a consultant would not leave a strategy document without an outside-in bracket, and the inputs are already on p3: technology services revenue "$2.0 billion in 2025", "hundreds of clients globally across 70 countries", JPMorgan at $25.6 million. Change: add a half-page "Order of magnitude" box: revenue per client from those two public numbers, where JPMorgan sits against it, and a triangulated API base from Woodhead's "hundreds of Snowflake accounts, tens of thousands of pipelines" (p5). State the ranges and the assumptions; precision is not the point, having a number to argue with is.

15. **p14, Ex 17 measures have no baseline or target.** Current measures include "Named references in use", "One page adopted", "Time to answer the API question in an RFP"; Ex 18 Q1 then admits "The baseline for every measure in Exhibit 17" is unknown. Why: a measure without a baseline is not a measure. Change: give each win a public proxy baseline (today: zero named Studio references, zero earnings-call mentions, no public AI-connector description) and a target with a date (three named references in two quarters; Studio named in the next investor deck; a public Copilot-model description before the next Build vs. Buy webinar).

16. **p13, Finding 4 overreaches from its sample.** The headline is "Buyers say they want a core platform plus their own builds", resting on polls of a BlackRock webinar's attendees with "Attendee counts were not stated". Why: self-selected viewers of a vendor's webinar are not "buyers"; the finding is directionally right but the headline claims more than the evidence. Change: headline "Attendees of BlackRock's own webinar say..." or add a second, independent source; the quotation from the Alpha FMC partner (RFPs now ask about the API suite) is actually the stronger evidence and should lead.

### E. Recommendations

17. **p14, Ex 17 has owners and measures but no priority, effort, cost, timing or sequence.** The six wins are listed in finding order, not value order, and "quick" is asserted, not shown. Why: a named-proof programme (client legal approvals), an investor-relations metric (disclosure governance) and a public description of the Copilot plugin model (a product roadmap decision) are quarters of work; a written one-pager, a "what is in the box" page and an RFP answer are weeks. Change: add impact and effort ratings and a "when" column; split the table into "This quarter" (QW3 one-pager, QW6 in-the-box, QW4 RFP answer) and "Programmes" (QW1 named proof, QW2 API metric, QW5 Copilot description); mark the dependency that QW3 is the source text for QW4 and QW6.

18. **No one-page roadmap.** Why: six moves across marketing, product, solutions engineering, IR and compliance with no sequence will not be run. Change: a single page, two quarters wide, one row per win, with owner and the first visible output per month.

19. **No client segmentation behind the wins.** Ex 1 and Ex 3 give user personas, and QW1 implicitly segments (public buyers first), but no exhibit says which client segments the wins target and how many sit in each. Change: a short cut of the base, for example public buyers on the record, filers who already disclose Aladdin, existing Aladdin Enterprise clients with Data Cloud, prospects in active RFPs, with the win that applies to each and a rough count from public sources.

### F. Risks and the hypothesis

20. **pp14–15. There is no "what would change my view" section; Ex 18 is five questions, not risks.** Why: the risks are already in the body as facts: the Access.Internal tag on every specification (p6) could make QW6 impermissible; the 10-K pricing terms (p10) could make QW2 moot; client confidentiality norms could stall QW1. Change: convert Ex 18 into a risk table with three columns: the question, the finding or win it could overturn, and what the document would recommend instead. Add a one-line "I would change my view if..." for each of the four findings.

21. **The implicit hypothesis is not stated or tested against its alternative.** The document assumes Studio's low profile is an oversight to be fixed; the alternative is that it is a choice, because Studio is sold as part of Aladdin Enterprise rather than as a product with its own revenue line. The document itself reaches "The choice is deliberate" for the Copilot access model (p12) but does not apply the same test to Studio's naming. Why: the two readings lead to different recommendations (visibility moves vs enablement moves). Change: state the hypothesis on p2, give both readings, and say which evidence discriminates: Ex 12's zero count supports "choice"; the 2026 "Senior Product Director, Aladdin Studio Developer" posting (p8) and the RFP question support "oversight now worth fixing".

### G. Facts, inferences, recommendations: keep

22. **Keep the fact/inference/recommendation hygiene; it is the document's strongest feature.** The P/S/T grading, the "What it suggests about direction" callouts, the "author's proposals" label on Ex 17, the "Zero counts are reliable; other counts are approximate" caveat on Ex 12, and the disclosed corrections on p2 and p18 are better practice than most consulting decks. One trim: the sentence "The conclusions may be wrong for reasons the author cannot see" appears three times (p2, p15, p18). Once is honesty; three times reads as lack of conviction. Keep it on p18 only.

### H. Presentation

23. **Citation density impairs reading.** Inline superscript strings such as "3, 4, 5, 6" (p2) and "11, 7, 9, 10" (p14), and underlined links in nearly every sentence, give the body the texture of a legal brief. Change: one source line per exhibit and per paragraph, superscripts only on direct quotations and contested numbers; the register stays as is.

24. **The document cannot be presented in 20 minutes as it stands, and it does not offer a path.** 18 exhibits over roughly 12.5 content pages. Change: either add a "If you have 20 minutes" line on p2 naming the seven pages to read (p2, the new 17x chart, Ex 7, Ex 10, Ex 12, Ex 14, Ex 17 with roadmap), or restructure so that the summary plus the four finding headlines plus the roadmap is the presenting deck and the rest is the leave-behind.

25. **Layout faults.** p12 is half empty after Ex 15; the heading "What I would need from inside" on p14 is separated from its exhibit on p15; Ex 14 breaks across pp11–12; Ex 3 carries "For illustrative purposes only" on a diagram built from cited sources, which undercuts it. Change: reflow so each exhibit sits with its heading, and drop the disclaimer on Ex 3 or move it to the About page.

26. **Keep: exhibit numbering, typography, footer, the consistent callout format.** Exhibits run 1 to 18 in order with a source line on each; the type system is consistent and the Studio rows are highlighted where it helps (Ex 1, Ex 7). This is the presentational base to build on, not to redo.

## Grade

As a work sample for a strategy role: **B+**. Evidence and intellectual honesty: A-. Argument architecture (pyramid, MECE, action titles): B-. Quantification and prioritisation: C+. Presentation: B. The partner's one-line review: "Excellent evidence, no argument on top of it. The answer is on page 2, paragraph three; the risks are on pages 6 and 10, unlabelled; the sizing inputs are on page 3, unused. One more pass and this is a client document." For the PMM role specifically, the findings show the candidate can locate insight and turn it into positioning; the gap is the strategy wrapper, which is the part an MBA reader weights most.

## The three changes to make first

1. **Pyramid it.** Rewrite p2 as answer-first: one governing sentence, three business-number tiles (17x, 0 of 26, 7 vs 0), the four finding headlines, the six wins as a numbered list with owner and date. Add the Part 2 label, cut Part 1 to two pages of "the outside view", and move Ex 1, 2, 4, 6 and the duplicate of Ex 8/9 to an appendix. The document drops to about 12 pages and presents in 20 minutes.

2. **Prioritise and de-risk the wins.** Give Ex 17 impact, effort and timing columns, split it into "this quarter" and "programmes", add a one-page two-quarter roadmap, and convert Ex 18 into a risk table that names the finding each risk would overturn, starting with the 10-K pricing terms against QW2 and Access.Internal against QW6. State the hypothesis (oversight vs choice) and which evidence decides it.

3. **Put numbers in.** Chart the 17x against 4x, 2x and 1.3x; pull the three contract values from the tender and USAspending notices already cited; add an outside-in order-of-magnitude box from the $2.0 billion, "hundreds of clients" and $25.6 million figures already in the text; give every measure in Ex 17 a public proxy baseline and a dated target; rewrite the remaining exhibit titles as the so-what, and let Ex 7 say what its own total says.
