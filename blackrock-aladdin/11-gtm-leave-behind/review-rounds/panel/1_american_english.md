# Copy-edit review: American English, style and usage

Reviewer perspective: senior copy editor, US business publication.
Document: "Aladdin Studio, learned with AI," 18 pages, as of October 19, 2026.
Scope: author's prose only. Quoted transcript material (lowercase "Aladdin data cloud," "Aladdin studio estate," "copilots," "plug-ins," "end to end life cycle," "reevaluating," "in house," "specialized in-house technology," "Build vs buy") is verbatim and was left alone, as the brief requires.

## Verdict

The document is already written in American English and is far cleaner than most drafts of this length: US spellings throughout (catalog, license, program, organization, specialized, authorized), US date format on every date (October 19, 2026), double quotation marks with no single quotes, numerals with commas, dollar figures in US form, and the AP convention of "percent" in prose and "%" in charts. What remains is a short list of leaks and inconsistencies that a US business reader would notice: two uses of "programme," one "panellist," two "mis-heard," British "agree one measure with" (US: "agree on"), three commas and a period that sit outside closing quotation marks, "US$" on a chart aimed at a US reader, lowercase "id/ids" where the same page uses "ID," abbreviated month names in two tables where the rest of the document spells months out, a wobbling serial comma, and a handful of first-reference problems (Sewdass, Newman, Goel, Rosales and Valdez appear by surname before they are introduced). A few sentences are hard to parse on first read or use a word that means something else to a US finance reader ("go public"). None of this is structural; all of it is fixable in an hour.

## Findings

### A. British or non-US spelling and usage

1. **Page 14, Exhibit 17, first row.** Current: "A named-proof programme, starting where the relationship is already public." British spelling. Change to "A named-proof program, starting where..."

2. **Page 15, Exhibit 18, second row.** Current: "How soon the named-proof programme can start." Same. Change to "program."

3. **Page 13, paragraph 1.** Current: "and the Alpha FMC panellist said a transformation runs 'roughly 12 to 18 months'." British double-l. Change to "panelist."

4. **Page 2, "What this method can and cannot do" box, and page 15, Source register preamble.** Current: "names and jargon can be mis-heard" (twice). Merriam-Webster and AP: "misheard," no hyphen. Change both.

5. **Page 14, Exhibit 17, "Studio's name on the API growth story" row.** Current: "Agree one measure with investor relations and product." Transitive "agree a measure" is British usage; a US reader expects "agree on." Change to "Agree on one measure with investor relations and product, built on..."

6. **Page 14, Exhibit 17, first row.** Current: "Shortlist clients whose Aladdin relationship is on the record." "Shortlist" as a verb is more British than American, though Merriam-Webster accepts it. Low priority. If changed: "Draw up a short list of clients whose Aladdin relationship is on the record."

7. **Page 7, Exhibit 7 legend.** Current: "1: Partial or after signup or approval." As a noun, AP and Merriam-Webster prefer "sign-up." Change to "after sign-up or approval."

8. **Page 10, paragraph 2.** Current: "across commitments, fundings, pay-downs and PIK." US finance usage is "paydowns" (Merriam-Webster: paydown). Change. Also consider expanding PIK on first use: "payment-in-kind (PIK) interest."

9. **Page 9, Exhibit 11 source line; page 15 register entries 11 and 50; page 16 entry 51.** Current: "not re-pulled" (three times) and "re-read" (page 9 source line and register entry 51). US style closes most re- compounds: "reread." "Re-pulled" is awkward either way; suggest "not refreshed" or "not pulled again." The document already writes "rebid" closed (page 9, Exhibit 11), so "reread" matches.

10. **Page 13, Exhibit 16, left chart label.** Current: "Actively re-evaluating." The author's own prose two paragraphs above quotes the poll option as "actively reevaluating" (US spelling, closed). The chart label should match the quoted option: "Actively reevaluating."

11. **Pages 9, 10 and 15: "US" and "UK."** Current: "US Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation," "US-hosted," "UK Pension Protection Fund," "Nest (UK)," "UK Find a Tender," "US asset manager coverage." The document is internally consistent (no periods), which is Economist/FT/Bloomberg house style. AP and the Wall Street Journal write "U.S." and "U.K." This is a house-style choice; keep if the author prefers the no-period form, but note that a US business publication would expect periods. If changed, change all six and "US$" (finding 12) together.

12. **Page 9, Exhibit 10 title and y-axis.** Current title: "JPMorgan Chase payments to BlackRock for Aladdin, US$ millions." Current axis label: "US $ millions" (with a space). Two problems: the two labels disagree with each other, and "US$" is the international convention for distinguishing currencies; in a document prepared for a US reader about a US bank paying a US company, plain "$ millions" is the norm. Change both to "$ millions."

13. **Page 8, Exhibit 8 "Where" column and Exhibit 9 "Speaker, date" column.** Current: "Snowflake podcast, Nov 2025"; "Never Done, Jan 2025"; "Richard Newman, Jan 2025"; "Dave Woodhead, Nov 2025." Everywhere else the document spells months out ("November 11, 2025," "January 8, 2025," "September 2025," "August 2026"), and AP spells out a month that stands with a year alone. Change to "November 2025" and "January 2025." If the column is too narrow, use AP abbreviation with periods: "Nov. 2025," "Jan. 2025."

14. **Page 8, paragraph 1.** Current: "Exhibit 7 puts Studio beside ten platforms a Studio buyer also deals with." The document otherwise follows AP numerals for 10 and above ("11 PyPI packages," "14 releases," "26 earnings calls," "54 specifications"). Change "ten" to "10." The other spelled-out numbers (six steps, eight criteria, five speakers, four utility APIs, six other bids, three pension funds) are all under 10 and correct; "Eleven add-on packages" (page 5) is correct because it opens a sentence.

### B. Punctuation

15. **Page 3, Exhibit 1, Aladdin Studio row.** Current: "'API-first', for 'a professional engineer...'" Comma outside the closing quotation mark (British practice). US style puts it inside: "'API-first,' for 'a professional engineer...'"

16. **Page 11, paragraph 1.** Current: "a plugin registry with 'couple of thousands agents and tools', where the first way a team onboards is..." Same error. Change to "...agents and tools,' where..."

17. **Page 16, Source register entry 44.** Current: "Full-text search of SEC EDGAR for 'Aladdin Studio'. SEC EDGAR, run October 2026." Period outside the closing quotation mark. Change to "...for 'Aladdin Studio.' SEC EDGAR, run October 2026." (The rest of the register and all other quoted passages in the document correctly place commas and periods inside, e.g. "'Open,' 'Compl Pending,' 'Authorized,'" on page 4, so these three are stragglers.)

18. **Serial comma, document-wide.** The dominant practice is AP style, no serial comma: "not produced, reviewed or endorsed by BlackRock" (pages 1, 2 and 18 footers), "specifications, pages and recordings" (page 2), "documentation, sandboxes and credentials" (pages 2, 3, 14), "login, retries, rate limits, pagination and long-running operations" (page 5), "commitments, fundings, pay-downs and PIK" (page 10), "pulled the pages, unpacked the packages, transcribed the recordings and drafted" (pages 15, 18). But a serial comma appears in:
   - Page 2, "What was found": "APIs and events, a Python SDK on GitHub, and Aladdin Data Cloud on Snowflake." Defensible under AP because the first item contains "and"; but the same list on page 3 (Exhibit 1, Aladdin Studio row) reads "APIs and events, a Python SDK, Aladdin Compute and Aladdin Data Cloud" with no serial comma. Pick one.
   - Page 2: "JPMorgan Chase's proxy statements, Janus Henderson's commits and 10-K, Royal London's chief executive on video, and three pension funds' tender notices." Defensible for the same reason (an item contains "and").
   - Page 8, paragraph 2: "the catalog is unpacked from packages, the definitions transcribed from recordings, and nothing can be run." Remove the comma before "and" or keep consistently.
   - Page 15, Exhibit 18, last row: "awareness, the first API call, or getting a build into production?" Remove the comma before "or."
   - Page 18, "About this document": "press releases, job postings, and transcripts of BlackRock recordings." Remove the comma before "and."
   Suggested rule: keep the AP default (no serial comma) and use it only where a list item itself contains "and" (the two page 2 cases). Fix pages 8, 15 and 18. The serial comma in "Model Context Protocol (MCP), AI agents, and Copilot extensions" (pages 11 and 12) is inside a quotation from the job posting and stays.

19. **Page 6, paragraph after Exhibit 5.** Current: "Ten specifications expose a long-running-operations endpoint and 35 operations are flagged for it; the SDK polls them." Two independent clauses joined by "and" need a comma: "...endpoint, and 35 operations are flagged for it." Also "a long-running-operations endpoint" stacks three hyphens; "an endpoint for long-running operations" reads better.

### C. Capitalization and product names

20. **Page 4, Exhibit 2, step 5, and page 5, Exhibit 4, first row.** Current: "open and close transaction ids" and "Every public specification carries an id of the form agraph.<domain>..." Lowercase "id/ids" in prose; page 6 correctly writes "the client ID and secret." Change to "IDs" and "an ID of the form." (Field names such as oborLotId stay as written.)

21. **Aladdin Compute vs. Studio Compute.** Page 3 (Exhibit 1), page 5 (Exhibits 3 and 4) call it "Aladdin Compute"; page 7 (Exhibit 6, "Try" row) says "Studio Compute"; the quoted README says "Aladdin Studio Compute sessions." Author prose should use one short form. Suggest "Aladdin Compute" everywhere outside quotations, since that is what the product page and Exhibit 1 use.

22. **ADC, page 5 and page 6.** Current: "security creation and ADC datasets" (Exhibit 4) and "Risk exceptions and ADC datasets carry bitemporal fields" (page 6). The abbreviation is never expanded. On first use write "Aladdin Data Cloud (ADC) datasets," then ADC.

23. **MCP, page 6.** Current: "the SDK's dependencies conflict with the official MCP toolkit." First use of MCP in the document; it is expanded only on page 11 ("Model Context Protocol (MCP)"). Expand at first use on page 6 or write "the official Model Context Protocol toolkit."

24. **Page 14, Exhibit 17, Owner column.** Current: "Marketing, with the client business for introductions." Lowercase "the client business" reads as a generic phrase; the register (entry 10) shows it is a named unit, "the Aladdin Client Business." Capitalize as a proper name ("the Aladdin Client Business") or say "client-facing teams."

25. **Page 5, Exhibit 4, first row.** Current: "The 54 public specs include orders, trades..." Everywhere else the prose says "specifications"; "Specs" is fine as a column header in Exhibit 5 but not in running text. Change to "specifications."

26. **Copilot, plugin, Data Cloud, eFront, Preqin, JPMorgan, GitHub, PyPI, Snowflake, Databricks, OAuth: keep.** Author prose is consistent: "Aladdin Copilot"/"Copilot," "plugin" (registry, model, builder, Copilot plugins), "Aladdin Data Cloud"/"Data Cloud," "eFront," "Preqin," "JPMorgan Chase"/"JPMorgan," "GitHub," "PyPI." Lowercase "copilot," "plug-ins," "Aladdin data cloud" and "Aladdin studio estate" occur only inside quotations and must stay. "JP Morgan to connect to BlackRock's Aladdin" (register entry 46) is the publication's own headline and stays. "Blackrock Aladdin Explore: Intro" (register entry 25) is the uploader's title and stays.

27. **Page 15, Exhibit 18, and page 14, Exhibit 17.** "Forward Deploy Engineer" is the posting's title, so it stays capitalized when naming the posting, but see finding 36 for the modifier use.

### D. Hyphenation

30. **Keep:** "open-source SDK," "add-on," "one-page version," "read-and-write APIs," "code-curious," "near-real-time data store," "machine-transcribed," "US-hosted," "co-wrote," "self-serve," "go-lives," "what-if," "third-party," "plain-English," "named-proof," "no-advice guardrails," "right-hand column," "long-running operations" (noun phrase). ### E. Typos, grammar and first references

### F. Sentences a US business reader would find awkward

39. **Page 9, paragraph after Exhibit 11.** Current: "Public pension funds, and firms whose filings or tenders already name BlackRock, can go public more easily, and are the natural first candidates for a named Studio story." To a US finance reader "go public" means an IPO. Change to "can be named publicly more easily" or "can speak on the record more easily."

40. **Page 9, same paragraph.** Current: "in custom-application land, proof from one firm counts even for a dissimilar buyer." "-land" is colloquial for a BlackRock leave-behind. Change to "for custom applications, proof from one firm counts even with a dissimilar buyer."

41. **Page 9, finding 1 headline.** Current: "Public customer proof already exists, and it is viable even though it is not Studio-specific." "Viable" is an odd fit; the point is that the proof is usable. Change to "...and it is usable even though it is not Studio-specific."

42. **Page 3, paragraph 1.** Current: "BlackRock's technology services and subscription revenue was $2.0 billion in 2025, up 24 percent with Preqin." "With Preqin" is ambiguous (because of Preqin? including Preqin?). Change to "up 24 percent including Preqin" or "up 24 percent, a figure that includes Preqin."

43. **Page 11, paragraph 3.** Current: "Seven providers a Studio buyer also talks to offer a connector or an MCP server." Garden-path sentence: "talks to offer" reads as a verb phrase. Change to "Seven providers that a Studio buyer also deals with offer a connector or an MCP server." Same construction on page 8 ("ten platforms a Studio buyer also deals with") is tolerable but "10 platforms that a Studio buyer also deals with" is smoother.

44. **Page 9, paragraph after Exhibit 10.** Current: "In December 2025 a Janus Henderson engineer submitted two changes to the open-source SDK, URL rewriting and custom headers, for firms that route Aladdin calls through an enterprise gateway." The comma-set appositive makes "URL rewriting and custom headers" look like a third and fourth thing submitted. Use parentheses: "submitted two changes to the open-source SDK (URL rewriting and custom headers) for firms that route..."

45. **Page 10, paragraph 2.** The sentence beginning "BlackRock bought eFront in 2019 and Preqin in 2025, and in September 2025 its private markets team described the result on a client webinar: investors 'now have access...'" runs five lines through three semicolons before reaching "'our new colleagues from HPS.'" Split after "for benchmarking;" into two or three sentences. Also "described the result" is vague; "described what the two acquisitions added."

46. **Page 10, paragraph 2.** Current: "Newman lists eFront data and Preqin among what is joining Data Cloud." Change to "Newman lists eFront data and Preqin among the data sets joining Data Cloud."

47. **Page 10, paragraph 2.** Current: "Microsoft wrote in December 2024 that all BlackRock's technology platform clients and services are hosted in the Azure cloud." US idiom: "all of BlackRock's."

48. **Page 10, paragraph 1.** Current: "would grow 17 times by 2030, against four times for model portfolios, two times for portfolios and 1.3 times for trade allocations." Mixed forms; the page 2 tile says "17x." Either "17x ... 4x ... 2x ... 1.3x" or "17-fold ... fourfold ... twofold ... 1.3-fold." "Two times" in particular reads as nonidiomatic; "twice" or "2x."

49. **Page 8, paragraph 2.** Current: "Five BlackRock speakers define Studio on the record in the 128 recordings pulled for this document, with the titles the recordings give." The trailing clause dangles. Change to "Five BlackRock speakers define Studio on the record in the 128 recordings reviewed for this document; titles are as the recordings give them."

50. **Page 6 heading, page 6 paragraph and page 7 Exhibit 6 column header.** Current: "What is out there before a conversation starts," "records what is out there at each step," "Out there today." "Out there" is conversational for this register. Change to "What is publicly available before a conversation starts," "records what is publicly available at each step," and "Public today." Low priority if the author wants the plainer voice.

51. **Page 2, "How to read the receipts," and the "Receipt" column on pages 7, 11, 12 and 14.** "Receipts" in the sense of "proof" is internet slang that has crossed into business talk, but on a leave-behind for a BlackRock executive it reads informal. Keep if it is a deliberate voice marker (it is used consistently and is explained on page 2); otherwise "Sources" or "Evidence."

52. **Page 13, paragraph 3.** Current: "BlackRock's August 2026 insight says 'Build vs buy is no longer a binary question'." "Insight" as a countable noun for an article is marketing-speak. Change to "BlackRock's August 2026 'insights' page says..." or "an August 2026 BlackRock article says..."

53. **Page 14, Exhibit 17, last row, Measure column.** Current: "First meetings that start past scope." Unclear. Change to "First meetings that skip the scoping discussion."

54. **Page 14, Part 3 heading.** Current: "Based on this, the quick wins in marketing Studio." As a running head "Based on this" is weak and the cover and page 2 call the section "Quick wins for marketing Studio." Match the cover: "Quick wins for marketing Studio."

55. **Page 9, Exhibit 11, "Nest (UK)."** US readers will not know Nest. Consider "Nest (U.K. workplace pension)" in the cell or a short gloss in the source line. Avoid "scheme," the British term, which the document correctly does not use.

- "percent" in prose, "%" in the Exhibit 16 chart: correct AP practice.
- Double quotation marks throughout; no single quotes used as primary quotes.
- US spellings "catalog," "license," "program," "organization," "specialized," "authorized," "center" (none of "centre" in prose; the one "media-centre" is in an LSEG URL).
- "Toward/towards," "whilst," "amongst," "learnt," "per cent," "co-pilot": none in author prose.
- Dollar figures: "$2.0 billion," "$4.4 million to $7.6 million," "$25.6 million." Correct.
- "Q1 2020 to Q2 2026," "first quarter of 2020," "third quarter of 2025," "fiscal year 2025": fine.
- "Build vs. Buy webinar" with period on "vs.": AP-correct; the quoted page title "Build vs buy" without a period stays as the source wrote it.
- Ellipses as three spaced periods inside quotations: consistent.
- Em dash only in pull-quote attributions; no spaced en dashes in running prose.
- "learned with AI" in the title: US form (British "learnt").

## The three changes I would make first

1. **Fix the four hard British leaks and the three outside-the-quote punctuation marks** (findings 1 to 5, 15 to 17): "programme" x2, "panellist," "mis-heard" x2, "Agree one measure" to "Agree on one measure," and move the comma/period inside the closing quotation mark on pages 3, 11 and 16. These are the items a US editor spots in the first pass and that undercut a document whose whole pitch is care with detail.

2. **Name people on first mention** (findings 34 and 35): Alisha Sewdass on page 13 before the callout cites "Sewdass"; Richard Newman and Diwakar Goel with titles on pages 3 and 5 rather than page 8; Brennan Rosales and Pedro Vicente Valdez on page 5 or 11. A reader of a leave-behind should never have to turn to the register to learn who is being quoted.

3. **Rewrite the sentences that mislead or stall a US finance reader** (findings 39, 33, 43, 31, 32): "go public" (page 9), "1 one announced" (page 7 legend), "Seven providers a Studio buyer also talks to offer" (page 11), the eFront row missing its noun (page 3), and "places it 'part of Studio'" (pages 3 and 8). Then settle the serial comma (finding 18) and the "$ millions" chart label (finding 12) in the same pass.
