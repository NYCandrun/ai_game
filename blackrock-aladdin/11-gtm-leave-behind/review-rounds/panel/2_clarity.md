# Clarity review: can a busy executive follow each page in one pass?

Reviewer perspective: clarity editor. Test applied to every page: in ten seconds, can the reader tell what the page claims and why it matters; do the headings carry the point; do the exhibits earn their place and do their titles state findings; are there buried points, sentences over 30 words, undefined jargon, receipt clutter, or forward references.

## Verdict

Part 2 (pages 9 to 13) and the quick-wins table (page 14) pass the one-pass test: the four finding headings are sentences that carry the point, each finding ends in a "What it suggests about direction" callout, and Exhibit 17 gives an owner and a measure per win. The executive summary and Part 1 (pages 2 to 8) do not pass. The summary leads with the method ("an outsider with AI agents and about a day") rather than the claim, so a reader who gives it ten seconds learns how the document was made, not what it found; two of its three big-number tiles are effort counts, not findings; and it uses three terms it has not yet defined ("the gate", "the Copilot plugin model", "the spoken message"). Part 1 is six pages of exhibits whose titles are labels rather than findings (only 3 of the document's 18 exhibit titles state a finding), two of its pages carry no prose at all, its paragraphs put the point in the last sentence, and it carries the engineer-level detail (API ids, scope strings, field names, status values) that an executive will skip and a technical reader will not need in a leave-behind. Five sentences run over 45 words, the longest at 87. The enterprise-gating caveat is stated six times and the method disclaimer three times. None of this is hard to fix: the content is there, the sequencing and titling are what need work.

## Findings

### Page 2, executive summary

1. **The lede states the method, not the claim.** Current text: "An outsider with AI agents and about a day can assemble a detailed, sourced picture of Aladdin Studio from BlackRock's own specifications, pages and recordings, plus filings and code. This is what it looks like, what it says about direction, and the quick wins for marketing Studio." Why it matters: a head of go-to-market reading the bold line gets a description of the exercise; the actual thesis (Studio is the product behind the growth story BlackRock tells investors, the story never names it, and six quick wins close that gap without opening the gate) is split across the first sentence of "What it says about direction" and the last sentence of "The quick wins". Six quick wins close both gaps and none opens the gate. This is what public sources, read with AI agents in about a day, show."

Suggested change: either make all three findings (17x API-call growth by 2030; 0 mentions of "Studio" on 26 earnings calls; $25.6 million JPMorgan Chase paid for Aladdin in 2025) or keep the current three and label the row "What was read" so the reader does not look for a claim in them. 3. **Receipt clutter in the summary.** Current text: "described the same way by five BlackRock speakers since 2024 3, 4, 5, 6" and "three pension funds' tender notices 7, 8, 9, 10, 11". 4. **"None opens the gate" arrives before the gate is defined.** Current text: "a 'what is in the box' summary. None opens the gate." Why it matters: on page 2, "Exhibit 6" means nothing; the sentence reads as a defence against an objection the reader has not formed. Suggested change: cut the sentence from the box; it is already repeated on page 6 next to the exhibit.

7. **"Three corrections already made" appears twice, verbatim.** Page 2 box and page 18 "About this document". Why it matters: it costs reading time on the one page meant to be fast, and the repetition makes the reader suspect the document is padded. Suggested change: keep it on page 18; on page 2 reduce to one line: "Three figures from earlier drafts were corrected; see page 18."

8. **Does the summary stand alone? Nearly.** It gives what Studio is, that proof exists, four direction points and six wins. It lacks a single thesis sentence (finding 1 above), uses three undefined terms (finding 5), and the "What it says about direction" paragraph is four unconnected findings in four sentences. ## The three changes I would make first

1. **Rewrite the executive summary so it carries the claim and stands alone.** Lead with the thesis (Studio is the product behind the growth story, the story never names it, the proof is public but not Studio-specific, six wins close the gap without opening the gate); make the three tiles findings; strip the superscripts from the summary paragraphs; replace "the gate", "the spoken message" and "the Copilot plugin model" with plain phrases; remove the Exhibit 6 forward reference and the duplicated corrections note.

2. **Retitle every exhibit as a finding and give every page a lead sentence.** Fifteen titles to rewrite (proposed wording above); one line of prose at the top of pages 5 and 7; move the Exhibit 7 explanation above the exhibit; merge Exhibits 8 and 9; cut Exhibit 13 to three steps so Exhibit 14 fits on one page.

3. **Put the point first in Part 1 and cut the engineer-level detail.** Open the page 3, page 6 (both paragraphs) and page 8 paragraphs with their conclusion; split the five sentences over 45 words (pages 3, 6, 9, 10, 13); reduce Exhibit 2's fourth column and Exhibit 4's id pattern to plain words; define MCP, IBOR/ABOR, scopes, Aladdin Graph and Explore at first use; state the gating caveat twice instead of six times.
