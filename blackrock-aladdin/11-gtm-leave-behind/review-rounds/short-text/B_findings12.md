# Short-text readability, pages 10 and 11 (Findings 1 and 2, Exhibits 10 to 12, direction callouts)

Scope: every heading, exhibit title, column header, cell, legend, axis label, source note and callout on pages 10 and 11, plus the Finding 2 direction callout, which sits at the top of page 12. Items are in page order. Text is quoted from the HTML; superscripts are shown as [n] and links are noted where they exist. "OK" items are listed briefly so the caller knows they were checked.

## Page 10

### 1. Finding 1 headline (h2)

**Current:** "1. Public proof that clients use Aladdin already exists, and it can serve as Studio proof even though none of it names Studio"

**Trying to say:** The public record of clients using Aladdin is good enough to stand as Studio's proof.

**Problem:** Three ideas in one line (proof exists; it works for Studio; none of it names Studio), joined by "and" and "even though". The reader has to unpack a twist before knowing the finding. The author's own example.

**Rewrite:** "1. The public record of clients using Aladdin can serve as Studio's proof"

**Where the dropped idea goes:** "None of it names Studio" is already the first sentence of the body ("BlackRock's Studio page names no client building on Studio[13], and a full-text search of SEC EDGAR finds no filing that mentions Studio[44]"), so nothing is lost. If the author wants the twist kept in the heading, the shortest honest form is "Studio has public proof already; it is filed under Aladdin's name" but that still asks the reader to unpack it, so I would not.

### 2. Exhibit 10 title

**Current:** "Exhibit 10. JPMorgan Chase paid BlackRock under $8 million a year for Aladdin from 2017 to 2023, then $14.7 million in 2024 and $25.6 million in 2025"

**Trying to say:** JPMorgan's Aladdin payments were flat for years and then jumped.

**Problem:** 25 words, three numbers and two date ranges; the reader has to hold "under $8 million, 2017 to 2023" while reading "then $14.7 million ... and $25.6 million".

**Rewrite (16 words):** "Exhibit 10. JPMorgan Chase's payments for Aladdin rose from under $8 million a year to $25.6 million in 2025"

**Where the dropped ideas go:** $14.7 million in 2024 is already labelled on the chart, and the 2017 to 2023 range is already in the body sentence above the exhibit ("$4.4 million to $7.6 million a year from 2017 to 2023"). Nothing needs adding.

### 3. Exhibit 10 chart legend, second entry

**Current:** "Integration services" (grey swatch), beside "Payments for Aladdin" (yellow swatch)

**Trying to say:** The one grey bar (2016) is a payment for integration services, not a license payment.

**Problem:** A fragment with no subject; the reader has to find the source note to learn that it applies to one bar and that it is also a payment.

**Rewrite:** "Payment for integration services (2016 only)". If the legend must stay short: "Integration services (2016)".

### 4. Exhibit 10 source note, first sentence

**Current:** "Source: JPMorgan Chase proxy statements (DEF 14A), filed 2017 to 2026, by year of payment[2, 3]."

**Trying to say:** The filings run 2017 to 2026; the chart plots each payment in the year it was made (2016 to 2025).

**Problem:** Two date systems in one clause ("filed 2017 to 2026" versus the x-axis 2016 to 2025); "by year of payment" is a fragment that does not say what is by year of payment.

**Rewrite:** "Source: JPMorgan Chase proxy statements (DEF 14A) filed 2017 to 2026[2, 3]. The chart plots each payment in the year it was made."

Rest of the note is fine. Optional tightening of the third sentence, which uses a colon to stack cause and claim: "JPMorgan is also a custodian, so the payments may cover more than a client license." Keep the link on "In 2016 it said it was connecting to Aladdin"[46] as is.

### 5. Exhibit 10 axis label and value labels

"$ millions", the year axis and the four bold value labels (4.4, 7.6, 14.7, 25.6): OK. One note: the bold labels mark 2017 and 2022 but not 2023, so a reader checking the title's "2017 to 2023" against the chart cannot see the 2023 value (6.9). If the title is rewritten as above this stops mattering.

### 6. Exhibit 11 title

**Current:** "Exhibit 11. Three public pension bodies scored Aladdin bids between 2017 and 2025, and Aladdin won every one"

**Trying to say:** Aladdin won every public pension tender on record.

**Problem:** Two ideas joined by "and" (the bodies scored bids; Aladdin won). The first half is set-up, not the finding.

**Rewrite (12 words):** "Exhibit 11. Aladdin won all three public pension tenders on record, 2017 to 2025"

**Where the dropped idea goes:** The scoring is what the table's third column shows, so the title does not need it. If the author wants it stated, add one sentence to the source note: "Each notice records how the bids were weighted or run."

### 7. Exhibit 11 column header, third column

**Current:** "How bids were weighted or run"

**Trying to say:** The basis on which each buyer judged the bids.

**Problem:** "weighted or run" stacks two meanings because one row (PBGC) has no weights. The reader pauses on "or".

**Rewrite:** "How bids were judged"

"Buyer", "Year", "Outcome": OK.

### 8. Exhibit 11 cells, third column, rows 1 and 2

**Current:** "Technical 85, price 6, price sensitivity 6, contract terms 3" and "Quality 70, cost 20, social value 10"

**Trying to say:** The percentage weights each buyer put on each criterion (both rows sum to 100).

**Problem:** Bare numbers with no unit and no verb; a reader at speed does not know these are percentage weights.

**Rewrite:** "Weighted technical 85%, price 6%, price sensitivity 6%, contract terms 3%" and "Weighted quality 70%, cost 20%, social value 10%"

### 9. Exhibit 11 cell, third column, row 3

**Current:** "Full and open competition, four bids each time"

**Trying to say:** PBGC ran an open federal competition both times and received four bids each time.

**Problem:** Minor. "Full and open competition" is the US federal procurement term, which a UK or non-procurement reader will not know; the second half has no verb.

**Rewrite:** "Run as a full and open competition (the standard US federal process); four bids were received each time"

### 10. Exhibit 11 cell, fourth column, row 1

**Current:** "Aladdin won both lots, at a price above the fund's own estimate"

**Trying to say:** The tender was split in two and Aladdin won both parts, bidding above the fund's estimate.

**Problem:** Minor. "Lots" has not been met in this sense; in Part 1 (the Accounting rows on pages 4 and 5) "lots" means accounting tax lots, so the word carries the wrong meaning here.

**Rewrite:** "Aladdin won both parts of the tender, at a price above the fund's own estimate"

### 11. Exhibit 11 cell, fourth column, row 3

**Current:** "Aladdin won in 2017 and again in 2023; the 2023 rebid kept the same price"

**Trying to say:** Aladdin won twice and the price did not change at the rebid.

**Problem:** Semicolon stacks two statements; "kept the same price" has an odd subject (a rebid keeping a price).

**Rewrite:** "Aladdin won in 2017 and again in 2023, at an unchanged price"

Row 2 outcome ("Aladdin won against six other bids"): OK. Year cells: OK. Source note ("Source: UK Find a Tender[51, 52]; USAspending.gov[53].") : OK.

## Page 11

### 12. Finding 1 direction callout

**Current:** "What this finding suggests about direction. Adoption is growing among client types whose records are public. Between 2023 and 2025, a bank's proxy statement, an asset manager's 10-K and three public pension buyers' notices all record Aladdin. Named proof for Studio can start where the relationship is already on the record."

**Label:** "What this finding suggests about direction." The word "direction" is set up on page 1 ("the likely initial directions") and in the Part 2 lede ("Part 3 sets out six directions"), and the label is the same in all four findings, so it reads as a fixed label. OK. If the author wants it more concrete: "What this points to for Part 3."

**Sentence 1.** "Adoption is growing among client types whose records are public." Trying to say: Aladdin adoption is now visible in public records. Problem: "client types whose records are public" is a compressed category the reader has to reconstruct. Rewrite: "Aladdin adoption is showing up in public records."

**Sentence 2.** OK as written. One fact check: "Between 2023 and 2025" fits the proxy (2024 and 2025 payments), the 10-K (Q3 2025) and the three notices (Nest 2023, PBGC rebid 2023, PPF 2025).

**Sentence 3.** "Named proof for Studio can start where the relationship is already on the record." Trying to say: start the named-client story with clients whose Aladdin use is already public. Problem: "the relationship" has no clear antecedent; "where" is a place standing in for a set of clients. Rewrite: "A named Studio story can start with clients whose Aladdin use is already on the record."

### 13. Finding 2 headline (h2)

**Current:** "2. BlackRock tells investors growth will come from API calls and the whole portfolio. Both run through Studio, though BlackRock rarely says the name"

**Trying to say:** The growth story BlackRock gives investors depends on Studio, but BlackRock rarely names it.

**Problem:** Two sentences and three ideas (two growth sources; both run through Studio; the name is rarely said). "The whole portfolio" is BlackRock's phrase and is only unpacked in the second body paragraph ("public and private markets together").

**Rewrite (12 words):** "2. BlackRock's growth story to investors runs through Studio, though it rarely says the name"

**Where the dropped idea goes:** Open the body with one sentence naming the two sources: "BlackRock tells investors growth will come from two things: API calls, and the whole portfolio, public and private markets together." The existing first sentence ("BlackRock projected at its 2025 Investor Day ...") then follows. Keep "rarely", not "never": the body records one 2023 Investor Day slide line[54].

**Trying to say:** Studio is never said on an earnings call.

**Problem:** 31 words and two sentences; the second sentence is a summary of the table, which the table already gives.

**Rewrite (15 words):** "Exhibit 12. No BlackRock executive said "Studio" on any of 26 earnings calls, Q1 2020 to Q2 2026"

**Where the dropped ideas go:** "Citizen developer" and "agent" at zero, and Data Cloud, API and Copilot at one or two each, are rows in the table. If the "about four times in total" line is wanted, add it to the source note after "My tally.": "The non-zero counts add up to about four mentions in six and a half years."

### 15. Exhibit 12 column header, third column

## Page 12 (top): Finding 2 direction callout

### 18. Finding 2 direction callout

**Current:** "What this finding suggests about direction. BlackRock itself says growth will come from extensibility and data access. The fastest-growing measure it showed investors is API calls. The data it is adding, private markets and Preqin, arrives through Data Cloud and Explore, the surfaces Studio provides. So Studio carries the growth story even when BlackRock does not say its name."

**Sentence 1.** "BlackRock itself says growth will come from extensibility and data access." Trying to say: BlackRock's own growth story rests on API use and data. Problem: "extensibility" has not been used in Finding 2; it appears once on page 9, inside a quoted job posting ("Aladdin's extensibility layer"[43]). "Data access" is also not the body's phrase. The body says API calls and the whole portfolio. Rewrite: "BlackRock itself says growth will come from API calls and from the whole portfolio."

**Sentence 2.** OK.

**Sentence 3.** "The data it is adding, private markets and Preqin, arrives through Data Cloud and Explore, the surfaces Studio provides." Trying to say: the new private markets data comes in through two Studio parts. Problem: two appositives stacked ("private markets and Preqin"; "the surfaces Studio provides"), and "surfaces" is a word used nowhere else in the document. Rewrite, two sentences: "The private markets data it is adding, from eFront and Preqin, arrives through Data Cloud and Explore. Both are parts of Studio." Fact check for "parts of Studio": Newman says Data Cloud "is part of Studio"[15] (Exhibit 9), but Part 1 presents Explore as an Aladdin application (page 5, the app row beside Dashboard and Risk Radar), not as a Studio part. Calling Explore a Studio surface is therefore the author's inference, and the safer close is: "The private markets data it is adding, from eFront and Preqin, arrives through Data Cloud and Explore. Data Cloud is part of Studio."

**Sentence 4.** "So Studio carries the growth story even when BlackRock does not say its name." OK; "even when" is one twist but the sentence is short and it closes the finding. Optional: "So Studio carries the growth story, named or not."

## The five changes that matter most

1. **Finding 1 headline (item 1).** Replace the three-clause sentence with "The public record of clients using Aladdin can serve as Studio's proof". The dropped clause is already the first sentence of the body.
2. **Exhibit 12 title (item 14).** Cut to "No BlackRock executive said "Studio" on any of 26 earnings calls, Q1 2020 to Q2 2026"; the rest is the table.
3. **Finding 2 headline (item 13).** One sentence: "BlackRock's growth story to investors runs through Studio, though it rarely says the name", with the two growth sources moved into a new first body sentence.
4. **Exhibit 11 title and weights cells (items 6 and 8).** Title becomes "Aladdin won all three public pension tenders on record, 2017 to 2025"; the two weights cells get "Weighted" and "%" so the reader knows what the numbers are.
5. **Finding 2 callout, sentences 1 and 3 (item 18).** Drop "extensibility and data access" and "the surfaces Studio provides", neither of which the reader has met, for the body's own terms: API calls, the whole portfolio, Data Cloud and Explore as parts of Studio.
