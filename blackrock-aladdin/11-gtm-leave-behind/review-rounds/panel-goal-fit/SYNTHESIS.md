## 2. What she will think

After reading alone, all six expect a positive reaction. Hiring manager: "rigorous, works with receipts, honest about limits, can write," a hire signal at VP. Sales: he knows where the gate is and quotes her colleagues accurately. Recruiter: "spent a day on us with AI, knows what he does not know, and writes like a grown-up." The editor calls the honesty about limits the quality you cannot interview for. The devil's advocate: "thorough, honest, slightly too much."

After forwarding, every reader moves the risk to the colleague who reads cold. The hiring manager names the Studio product lead or the engineer who merged PR 52, who sees a scorecard on people who were not in the interview. Sales splits it by desk: engineering stops on "the layer Copilot runs on," solutions engineering resents Exhibit 2, the Copilot team winces at "GPT-4," the client business rejects the Janus Henderson line, investor relations dislikes the 0, brand reads the cover as a consultant's deliverable. The recruiter expects the forward to reach the people quoted, who will check their machine-transcribed quotes first, and observes that hiring managers forward what makes them look smart and file what makes their team look bad. The devil's advocate adds legal, brand and client relationship owners. The PMM states the shared point: the body is fair each time, but headlines and one-number tiles are what a forwarded reader takes in.

## 3. What works against the goal

"Prepared for BlackRock," p.1 (6 of 6). Strongest version, editor: it was prepared for the candidate's own preparation, which is the whole charm, and the line turns a gift into a pitch. The devil's advocate adds that the per-page disclaimer contradicts it rather than undoing it. No dissent.

The p.2 stat tiles, especially "0 mentions" (6 of 6 on the zero; hiring manager, sales and devil's advocate on the row). Editor: it scores her company, out of context, in the largest type on the page, while Exhibit 12 frames the same number fairly. On 17x, five readers note the headline rests on the author's own inference; sales is the partial dissent, crediting the candidate for flagging it.

Exhibit 2, p.4 (6 of 6). Devil's advocate: a scoreboard with admitted bad data, where her product loses, sent to her; the text says selling model, the picture says "you are closed." The Janus Henderson engineer identified from a commit address, p.10, and "Janus Henderson first," Exhibit 16 (5 of 6). Recruiter: at Aladdin client confidentiality is the religion, a GTM head reads this as a blind spot, and it is the one passage that could cost him the role on a forward. Sales and the PMM add that the client's 10-K calls the migration "a complex, multi-year initiative." The editor dissents, citing the line as specificity where it counts.

The typeface colophon, p.16 (6 of 6 mention; four say remove). Hiring manager: it tells her the document was dressed to look like hers, honest and exactly the thing not to point out. No defence.

Stale source note 28 (4 of 6: hiring manager, recruiter, editor, devil's advocate). It cites a 3,000 citizen-developer figure Exhibit 3 no longer carries; the recruiter calls it the one tell that AI drafted and a human cut without re-checking.

"GPT-4 function calling," Exhibit 13 (2 of 6: sales, devil's advocate). The Copilot team will wince at it in print, public talk or not.

Stability columns, Exhibit 9 (1 of 6: devil's advocate, who reads the alpha/beta/stable inventory as reconnaissance). The other five praise the stability count by name, so the evidence argues against this removal.

"The layer Copilot runs on," p.2 (2 of 6: sales, recruiter). Sales: Rosales put "a couple of thousand" tools against 301 public operations, so most Copilot tools sit on APIs Studio does not expose; an insider will quote it back.

Finding 3's headline, "works only inside the login" (4 of 6: hiring manager, sales, PMM, devil's advocate). The Sewdass quote on p.14 is the vendor's own answer, quoted and not connected.

"I would describe the Copilot plugin model publicly now," p.13 (4 of 6: hiring manager, PMM, recruiter, devil's advocate). PMM: publishing plugin architecture is a security and legal call. The editor dissents and calls it a real opinion.

Part 3 unranked (5 of 6, all but sales). Recruiter: she finishes p.15 asking "so which one first?", which puts the most important sentence outside the document.

Smaller: "prospects stop raising connectors" as a measure nobody tracks (PMM, recruiter, devil's advocate; the hiring manager called it real); p.5 "Learning about it from outside takes effort" as lecturing (recruiter, devil's advocate); Exhibit 14 resting on a job posting (hiring manager, recruiter); the symmetrical "What this finding suggests" boxes as the main AI tell (hiring manager, sales, PMM, editor); "deepest implementation" unsourced (PMM); "128 recordings" without a receipt (recruiter).

## 4. The one change

- Hiring manager: rewrite pp.1 and 2 as personal notes; drop the cover line, replace the tiles with the three-part definition, disclaimer on the About page only.
- Sales: drop the cover line, subhead "notes from learning Studio from outside," open with 42 percent and 53 percent instead of 17x, $25.6M, 0.
- PMM: make Part 3 carry a sequence; RFP answer first, what it proves in 90 days, defer the rest.
- Recruiter: rewrite the Janus Henderson material on p.10 and p.15.
- Editor: change the cover line.
- Devil's advocate: take the costume off: cover line, yellow, typeface note, three tiles; retitle the summary.

Four of six put the change on the cover and page 2, and every forwarding scenario begins there. As an edit list:

1. Cover: delete "Prepared for BlackRock." Replace with nothing or "Notes made to prepare for interviews, October 2026" (editor, recruiter).
2. Page 2: delete the three tiles. Open with the three-part definition (hiring manager) or the webinar numbers (sales); the editor's alternative is "301 public API operations in 54 specifications." Exhibit 12 keeps the earnings-call finding.
3. Page 2 lede: cut "and the layer Copilot runs on" (sales, recruiter); reconcile "four speakers" with p.5 and Exhibit 4 (hiring manager, devil's advocate).
4. Optional: footer disclaimer on the About page only (hiring manager); the editor and PMM would keep it everywhere.

The recruiter's and PMM's single changes are better treated as removals the author should also make, since five readers back each.
