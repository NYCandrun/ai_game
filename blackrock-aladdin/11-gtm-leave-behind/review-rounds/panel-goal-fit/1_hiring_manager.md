# Hiring-manager read: "Aladdin Studio, learned with AI"

## 1. Does it show the three things?

**Learns a gated product from outside, with AI, and says where it stops: 5.** This is the strongest part, and the part I cannot check in an interview. Exhibit 1 (p.3) is the honest boundary: six steps, what is public, what opens after qualification, "I did not test it." Exhibits 6, 8 and 9 (pp.7 to 9) go further than most of my own PMMs would go in a quarter: 54 specs unpacked from 11 PyPI packages, stability labels counted, OAuth scope strings quoted, whatIf and runCompliance read off the spec, one trade followed from order to NAV. The hedges are the right kind, and nothing claims to have run anything.

**Reads the business: 4.** JPM's proxy payments (Exhibit 10), the three tenders (Exhibit 11), the earnings-call tally (Exhibit 12), the AI-access table (Exhibit 14) and the webinar polls (Exhibit 15) are the right sources, and I have not seen a candidate assemble them. One point off because the two numbers he leads with on p.2 are the weakest: 17x is his inference, as the caption admits, and "0 mentions on 26 earnings calls" is a count of what executives did not say, not a finding about the business. He also never says the obvious thing about Exhibit 2: a system of record gates on purpose, and she knows that.

**Has a point of view on the job: 3.** Exhibit 16 (p.15) has the right columns, produce, why, measure, each tied to a finding, and the measures are real ("time to answer the API section of an RFP," "prospects stop raising connectors in deal reviews"). But it is one page after fourteen of research, nothing is ranked, and four of the five are what any VP PMM lists on day one: RFP answer, named reference, use-case page, flagship piece. The one with an edge, publishing the Copilot permission model as a governance story, is the only place he says something the product page does not already imply. The job is judgment; this document is ninety percent evidence by weight.

## 2. Help or hurt as a follow-up?

Net help, with a forwarding cost. After reading it she thinks: rigorous, works with receipts, honest about limits, can write. That is a hire signal at VP.

If she forwards it to the Studio product lead, or to the engineer who merged PR 52, the reaction is different. That reader sees a cover that says "Prepared for BlackRock," a scorecard where Aladdin sits at 4 beside Snowflake at 16 with four bold zeros (Exhibit 2), a headline that their AI "works only inside the login" (p.12), a client's engineer traced by email domain in commits (p.10), and a Mumbai associate job posting as the sole evidence of their MCP position (Exhibit 14). None of it is wrong. All of it is a scorecard on people who were not in the interview, and she will hesitate before forwarding.

## 3. What works against the goal

- "Prepared for BlackRock" on the cover, and "Not produced, reviewed or endorsed by BlackRock" on every page. The first presumes a deliverable; the second is the language of something meant for publication. Both contradict "whipped up to prep."
- The hero row on p.2 opens with an inference, a client's spend and a zero. A first-time reader meets a knock before they meet the product.
- Exhibit 2 scores Aladdin on a rubric the caption admits is unreliable ("Bloomberg, Clearwater and MSCI may score lower than they deserve"). A scored table with a broken method is worse than no table.
- Finding 3's headline. Sewdass's own line on p.14, "you need that system of record that underpins anything that AI is built upon," is the answer to it. He quotes her and keeps the headline.
- "I would describe the Copilot plugin model publicly now" (p.13) and "Janus Henderson first" (p.15) are the voice of someone already in the chair.
- Editing seams: p.2 says four speakers defined Studio, p.5 says five, Exhibit 4 says "all four"; source 28 refers to a 3,000 citizen-developer figure that Exhibit 3 no longer shows; "Aladdin Compute" in Exhibit 8 against "Studio Compute" everywhere else. These are the marks of a drafted-then-trimmed text, and with the uniform sentence rhythm and the per-finding "What this finding suggests" boxes, they read as generated consultant format even though the content underneath is not.
- The typeface note on p.16 tells her the document was dressed to look like hers. Honest, and exactly the thing not to point out.

## 4. The single change

Rewrite pages 1 and 2 so the document opens as personal notes rather than a report to the company: drop "Prepared for BlackRock," replace the three hero stats with the three-part definition and a plain sentence on what the public record does and does not show, and keep the disclaimer on the About page only. Everything after that reads differently once the frame is right.

Remove before sending: Exhibit 2 entirely, and the typeface sentence on p.16. Soften "Janus Henderson first" to "a client whose engineers already contribute to the SDK."

## 5. Send as is?

Yes with one change: the research is the best outside-in read of a gated product I have seen from a candidate, but as sent it is framed as a deliverable to her company and opens by scoring her product, which is the one thing a "made this for myself" follow-up must not do.
