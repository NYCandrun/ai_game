# Market analyst's read

## 1. What the evidence indicates

**Direction.** BlackRock is converting Aladdin from an application suite into a data-and-API platform, and Studio is that layer. Exhibits 3 and 4 (five executives, one definition) and Exhibit 7 (one shared API layer under apps, Copilot and Studio) make this strong. The private-markets thread on page 11 (eFront, Preqin, HPS transaction types landing in Data Cloud and Explore) is strong and matters most: no system-of-record rival owns a private-markets data business.

**Position.** Exhibit 2 says Studio sells the way its category sells: 4 of 16, beside Bloomberg at 5 and State Street at 4. The gap to Snowflake and Databricks at 16 is a category difference, not a deficit, whatever the document implies. Exhibit 14 is the real gap: six data and cloud providers ship connectors for outside AI, SimCorp has announced client-built agents, Aladdin has a job advert. Moderate-strong on announcements, weak on adoption (LSEG's 90 customers is a secondary summary). Exhibit 9 is strong primary evidence that the catalog is younger than the "API-first" label: 24 of 54 specifications alpha, compliance mostly alpha, no public position or event API.

**Buyer.** Exhibit 15 draws the segment: two-thirds run vendor or hybrid stacks, 42 percent cite AI and data demands, 53 percent would hand off compliance reporting. Schafer (page 14) says the RFP question is now the API suite and Snowflake or Databricks connectors. Moderate: a self-selected BlackRock audience, unknown n. The SimCorp wins at AllianceBernstein and Pictet (page 14) are the share-shift signal: hybrid buyers keeping in-house tools, the buyer Sewdass describes. Exhibit 10 (JPMorgan, 6x since 2017) and the Janus Henderson pull requests show growth as expansion inside large, gatewayed accounts; strong on fact, n of two.

## 2. The strongest insight the document buries

Snowflake is on nearly every page and never treated as a competitor. Data Cloud is a tenant in a platform the client already buys; FactSet, LSEG, MSCI and S&P now deliver into the same place through MCP; Schafer's RFP question is about connectors to Snowflake and Databricks, not vendor tooling. Together, Exhibit 2's bottom half, Exhibit 14 and that quote say the build surface a Studio buyer chooses is the client's own cloud estate; Studio's job is to be the best-governed route into it, not the destination. The document also drops its one adoption number: Sources entry 28 says Goel and Woodhead cite 3,000 citizen developers; the figure is nowhere in the body.

## 3. Over-claimed, missing, wrong

- The 17x tile is the weakest number carried as the strongest. The slide is about the cost of growth; API calls are a unit-economics measure, BlackRock is Aladdin's largest user, and the 10-K ties fees to positions and fixed rates. Page 11 admits this and keeps the headline; the investor story is technology services revenue, up 24 percent.
- Zero earnings-call mentions proves nothing; sub-products of a $2 billion segment go unnamed.
- Exhibit 10 is confounded: JPMorgan pays as a custodian (Aladdin Provider) as well as a client; the footnote admits it, the title does not.
- Exhibit 11 cuts against its use: PBGC renewing at an unchanged price after six years is a pricing signal, not a win.
- Exhibit 2 is one day of scraping, equal weights, three vendors blocked, no Charles River or SS&C. Exhibit 14's Bloomberg "none" needs a second pass.
- Missing: Studio packaging and price, attach rates, client counts, win/loss against SimCorp, segment sizing. Before acting on Finding 4 I want the webinar's n and the AB and Pictet decision criteria.

## 4. First six months, marketing's remit

1. **First: reposition around the build surface.** Exhibits 2 and 14, the Schafer quote. A positioning document and battlecard placing Studio against SimCorp Agent Launchpad and the client's own Snowflake and Databricks estate, with Studio as the governed route into both. First because the AB and Pictet losses say the hybrid buyer is comparing now, and every asset below inherits it.
2. **Reference program with three named Studio stories.** Finding 1, Exhibits 10 and 11. Janus Henderson has disclosed in its 10-K, Royal London is on video, public pensions are easy to name; SimCorp names wins, Studio names none.
3. **Private markets as the lead use case.** Page 11. A Studio narrative, demo script and one flagship piece showing Preqin and eFront data reaching a client's Snowflake through Data Cloud: the story no system-of-record rival can tell, and the answer to the 42 percent citing AI and data demands.
4. **Developer-facing catalog with honest maturity labels.** Exhibits 6 and 9. A public catalog page showing the 11 domains with stability labels, and a messaging guardrail: no "end-to-end lifecycle" claim while trade and compliance are alpha.
5. **Publish the Copilot governance story and internal alignment on AI access.** Exhibits 13 and 14, PR 57. A governance explainer (who can call what, with which permissions, under which guardrails) marketing can ship now, plus an internal brief to product: eight of nine comparison vendors have announced outside-AI access and sales needs a stance.
