What Aladdin is and how it's built

Aladdin is the software big investors use to run their whole investment process on one system: deciding, trading, checking, settling and accounting. This brief shows the flow first, then the system that makes it possible.

Aladdin in 30 seconds

Who uses it

Institutional investors: asset managers, banks, insurers, pension plans and corporations16. BlackRock runs its own money on it too29.

What it replaces

A patchwork of separate order, risk, compliance and accounting systems. Clients describe moving away from "a number of different disparate systems"14.

Why that matters

Everyone works from one shared record of every portfolio, "a common language for portfolios"15. A trade is entered once and every team's view updates.

Words you will meet

IBOR
Investment book of record: the investment team's live view of what they hold.
ABOR
Accounting book of record: the official books the NAV is struck from.
NAV
Net asset value: a fund's value per share, published daily.
Block, allocation
One trade executed in bulk, then split across portfolios.
Cash ladder
Expected cash per currency for each coming settlement date.
Blotter
A trader's working list of orders.
Custodian
The bank that holds the assets and settles trades.
Corporate action, entitlement
A company event (dividend, merger, rights issue); the entitlement is what each portfolio is owed.
Warning vs restriction
A compliance warning passes but flags a rule; a restriction means the rule is breached.
VaR
Value at risk: the loss a portfolio should not exceed on most days.
Attribution
Explaining a return: how much came from sector choices and how much from picking securities.
Widget, workspace
A widget is one report on screen; a workspace is a saved layout of widgets.
Four-eyes
A change needs a second person's approval before it takes effect.
API
A programmatic way for software to read or change Aladdin data.
Tenant
One client's own separate copy of the system.

One trade, start to finish

Five teams touch every trade. The order moves left to right; once it is booked, four things happen at once from that same record. Step numbers are reused across the brief.

Order status Open · step 4→Compliance pending · step 5→Authorized, if step 5 passes→Active · step 6→Booked · step 7 the order API's own statuses22

  1. Before the trade

    1. 1Spot an idea · portfolio manager · research note or alert
    2. 2Test it in Explore what-if · portfolio manager
    3. 3Check cash on the cash ladder · portfolio manager
    4. 4Raise the order in Order Monitor · portfolio manager · Open
    5. 5Pre-trade check: passed → Authorized; warning flagged; a restriction stops the order (back to the PM is our inference) · automatic
    6. 6Execute and allocate · trader · Active · screen name inferred

  2. Booked on trade date

    1. 7The trade is booked. Everything below starts from this one record.

  3. After booking, in parallel

    1. 8Positions update in the live book (IBOR) · portfolio manager
    2. 9Post-trade compliance check · automatic
    3. 10Confirm with the broker desk · operations
    4. 11Settle and reconcile with the custodian · operations · reconciliation is our inference
    5. 12A lot opens in the official books (ABOR) · accounting
    6. 13NAV: exceptions, sign-off, publish · accounting

Steps and statuses come from the public API specs22. Explore2 and Order Monitor are named screens. The trader's screen is not named in any source. BlackRock describes the whole run as going "from that first order … into accounting without lifting up the pencil"9.

One trade, two books

The booked trade feeds two books at once. The IBOR is the investment team's live view of what they hold. The ABOR is accounting's official book, and the daily NAV is calculated from it.

Every record points back to two master lists

Portfolio

Mandate, benchmark, regulations and cash rules, with portfolios grouped into a hierarchy.

Security

One Aladdin ID per instrument, mapped to CUSIP, ISIN, SEDOL and others.

Checks attached along the way

Compliance

Rules checked on the order 5, again on the trade 9, and at start of day and intraday. Results: passed, warning or restriction.

Appendix

For engineers

How the public APIs are built, and what each design choice means for someone using the apps.

Public APIs per domain, by maturity

54 APIs and 301 operations in 11 packages, v2.0.0b322. The full catalog is behind a client login25.

stablebetaalphasunset

Conventions in every spec

  • Defined in protobuf and served as REST, with Google-style methods such as :filter and :batchCreate.
  • Two locks on each call: an OAuth scope such as trading…Order:write, then an Aladdin permission inside the service. Permissions attach only to user groups.
  • Each API is labelled alpha, beta, stable or sunset and versioned on its own.
  • Job postings describe a Kubernetes-based container platform underneath28 (our inference that it hosts Aladdin itself).
  • 190 of 301 operations are POST: filters travel in the body, batches are capped and long jobs return a task ID to poll.

How records behave, and what users notice

Behaviour read off the specs22. The "you see" lines are our inference.

History is kept

v2
v3

Rows carry entry and expiry times plus business dates, so any past state can be read back.

You see: an as-of date picker

Approval lives in the record

draft→review→approved

Rules, datasets and payments carry their own approval state; some need a second person.

You see: status chips and an approve button

Edits carry a version

A saves v5 ✓B saves stale v4 ✕

Orders and trades carry a touch count that rises with every edit; fund-flow records use it to reject stale updates.

You see: "someone else changed this"

Access by group

OAuth scope→Aladdin permission

Both must pass; Copilot's tools are filtered by user group the same way.

You see: only your portfolios and tools
