What Aladdin is

Aladdin is BlackRock's platform for running an institutional investment business end to end. One system holds the portfolios, the securities, the orders and trades, the rules they must obey, the cash and collateral they move, the accounting records they produce, and the risk views that sit on top. The product's value is that one booked trade updates every one of those views at once.

Who uses it

Asset managers, insurers, pension funds, wealth managers, banks and corporate treasuries, including Microsoft Treasury and soon Amazon Treasury. Institutional only.

What they pay for

A hosted, multi-tenant SaaS: each client gets its own Aladdin environment, with BlackRock staff able to be granted access into it as "provider" users.

From the 54 OpenAPI specs in BlackRock's public SDK plugin packages. Every field, state and name here is lifted from those documents, not from marketing.

Where AI plugs in

Aladdin Copilot sits across the apps as "connective tissue". Its tools are the same governed APIs Studio exposes, which is why API quality and AI readiness are one problem.

Federated ownership

The central AI team owns orchestration, guardrails and evaluation. Domain teams onboard by registering existing production APIs as tools or by building custom agents. That mirrors the domain split in the API namespace.

Evaluation-driven development

Synthetic data, subject-matter-expert cases and LLM-as-judge pipelines run on every pull request and nightly, with reports per plugin owner. Treated like test-driven development.

Why it matters for Studio

OpenAPI metadata, consistent custom methods and fine-grained scopes are what make an API usable as an agent tool. The Studio Developer product postings explicitly list AI-assisted tooling.

Test yourself

Click a card to reveal the answer. Then use the questions at the bottom in the interview; each one comes straight from something in the specs.

Questions worth asking them

  1. Of the public APIs, nearly half are alpha. How does an API graduate to stable, and how do you handle clients who built on alpha when it changes?
  2. Compliance and Risk Governance are two limit frameworks with separate rules, exceptions and workflows. Are they converging?
  3. What share of Studio usage is BlackRock's own teams versus clients, and what is the path from internal success to external adoption?
  4. Is the Copilot plugin registry fed from the same catalog as the Studio developer portal, or are they separate?
  5. Where is the line between exposing something as an API, as an ADC dataset, or as an event stream?
  6. The specs still cite legacy permission codes under OAuth scopes. Is the goal to retire those, or to keep two layers?
  7. What changes for Studio when a client is hosted on AWS rather than Azure?
