### Part 1. Aladdin

**1.1 What Aladdin is**
- Fitzgibbon's one-line definition: "the allowed in technology, which is BlackRock's investment and risk analytics platform" [051 line 28].
- Skinner on the iBOR as intraday picture: "at Aladdin, we talk a lot about the concept of an investment book, a record, or an I-bor having that intraday picture of what your exposure's risks and risks and performance are" [045 line 46].
- Skinner's definition of an iBOR: "you have to have accurate security masters and you have to have accurate transaction capture and the transactions then themselves create the positions in the cash movements. And then from there you structure workflows around those components" [056 line 78].
- Fitzgibbon on one system of record for all users: "rely on the iBOR, the A-BOR to really be the source of truth and the risk analytics and the performance and the portfolio manager and the risk team and the client are all receiving the analytics and the reports out of the same system" [051 line 74].
- Murgatroyd's platform framing: "Black Rocker Ladin is a technology platform that unifies the investment management process across all asset plazas [classes]" [056 line 14].

**1.2 Where it came from**
- Why eFront was bought, in Skinner's words: "I used to actually cover a series of our pension clients who are using the Aladdin platform here. And they had this problem ... they didn't have a great solution for sourcing data from the 100, 200 funds that they were LP investors in. So we made an acquisition in 2019 of a firm called E-Front and one of the, one of the nice things that we really liked about them was they had a managed data service. So they could go out and source this information from the GPs individually, collect it, bring it back in and at least normalize it" [045 line 68].
- Pre- and post-investment split of the two acquisitions: "In 2019 Black Rock acquired E-Front to underpin its private asset portfolio management technology and data services to drive post investment capabilities. More recently we acquired prequin, a private markets data and research company that is designed to fuel pre investment capabilities" [056 line 14]; "Over the past six years Black Rock has invested to expand its private markets capability, not just in asset management but also in technology and data" [056 line 14]; Faraz: "we started on this journey over six years ago to build a fully integrated end to end solution across public and private markets" [056 line 130].

**1.3 What it does**
- Explore demo contents (trade-tensions webinar): group-level summary across regions and funds, an alternatives view ("if I have any private assets in my portfolio, I can review them in a whole portfolio of your style"), "stress P and L alongside my risk and my total returns", heat map by sector, time series "in both portfolio and active spaces", interactive drill-down, custom sectors, "a much more disaggregated factor level", factor correlations and volatility over time [048 lines 48-68].
- Connectivity listed by Sewdass as commoditised rails: "the various connectivity via, you know, SWIFT and fixed [FIX] to counterparties and to brokers" [049 line 120].
- Exception-based monitoring, 2024 statement (the compendium has only the July 2026 one): "A major transformation that I've seen occur in the last couple of years and particularly within Aladdin is the shift toward exception based monitoring ... there's a lot of workflows behind the scenes that are running overnight on analytics and on portfolios or certain security exposures ... it's really been isolated to the point that a risk manager or portfolio manager can quickly react" [051 line 78].

**1.6 Risk and analytics**

- Stress-testing claim: "We have our proprietary risk models and analytics to stress test your portfolios against scenarios ... such stress testing is done through a bottoms up portfolio modeling approach and that allows you to have an integrated view of your exposures down to the asset and fund level across both public and private" [048 line 16].
- Scenario construction process in Aladdin: hypothetical event, "policy variable shots [shocks]" ("What Aladdin is going to do is take those policy variables and propagate those shocks throughout the rest of the market"), "the plausibility detail" ("is that likely or unlikely"), "perturbation measure implied shocks" ("Use the risk model and covariance matrix to imply shocks on all of the other underlying factors"), then portfolio analysis [048 lines 22-28].
- The US-Europe decoupling scenario: built "from March 6"; policy variables "a shock to volatility, the VIX index ... S&P outperforming MSCI Europe by 5% ... big divergence between the German tenure and the Treasury tenure"; output includes "the EMEA error and defense [aerospace and defence] factor with it with a large shock applied" [048 lines 32, 42-44].

- Economic scenario simulator, a named tool: "they were trying to. Optimize what their portfolio allocations look like in a strategic asset allocation exercise through the use of our economic scenario simulator. So what this does is this simulates economic paths out into the future, you know, going out as far as 30 years", followed by "the pacing model" and "the liquidity model" [056 lines 124-128].
- Private credit analytics: top-down "exposures and performance being able to model portfolio cash flows and various different returns and risk attributions"; bottom-up "probability of default loss given default being able to shock and make different assumptions around the draw downs of an unfunded commitment various different pay down assumptions on the loan" [056 line 92]; an insurance-liability example generating "an ex anti cash flow table for that entire portfolio of credit instruments across public semi liquid and purely private" and "producing economic shocks on the entire portfolio" [056 lines 94-100].
- Private credit risk differs from public: "the lender's not going to push the borrower into default. They're going to restructure that loan ... what is the probability of restructuring and what is the impact of those terms and my economics and my cash flows" [056 lines 150-152].
- Performance direction: "we're going to move more towards a total return type of performance metric across publics and privates rather than trying to migrate publics into like an IRR type of construct" [056 line 158], with daily carry and accrual modelled "for any type of loan facility" [056 line 170].
- Public/private alignment list (Skinner): "Think about data model, think about transaction model, think about risk factor framework and the calibration of those models. Think about how you're going to build a parsimonious performance based model, how you're going to choose, like which methodology for converting to time-weighted return you're going to use" [045 line 70]; private credit security master needs "conditionally draw term loans or revolvers" [045 line 74].
- Climate model mechanics: four steps plus one ("The very first step ... definition of the scenarios ... translating those assumptions into microeconomic impacts for us to be able to restate the entire balance sheet of the companies in our covered universe ... an updated free cash flow to equity that then flows into the Aladdin traditional asset evaluation models"); private securities via "a waterfall approach ... starting from a recording of public securities that are in the same geography or in the same subsector"; real assets "base methodology on the longitude and latitude where they are located" [047 lines 24-26]. Not catastrophe models: "the models are really Blackrock proprietary models. These are not cat models in the way insurance think of it ... ours are really asset evaluation models ... we don't do a stochastic modeling" and for equity "a dividend discount cash flow model with the climate adjusted cash flows" [047 lines 88-90].
- Climate demo numbers (MSCI ACWI vs S&P 500): coverage "99.9%" for physical and transition risk; "almost 4% for the physical risk component and almost 6% from the transition risk component"; "a 9.3% downward adjustment on the current value"; energy sector "almost 70% downward adjustment" in certain geographies; "only 84% of this portfolio is decarbonized by 2050" [047 lines 46-48, 50, 72].
- Risk is not only protection: "we also want to enable our users, our portfolio managers, to understand if they're taking on too little risk or not the right risks" [051 line 50]; private market risk models take "a customized and specific approach to different private market assets" [051 line 126].
