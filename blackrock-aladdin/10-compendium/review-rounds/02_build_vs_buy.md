**What it is about:** A BlackRock-hosted webinar, described on the page as "the first session of this asset manager webinar series" (line 8), in which Campos moderates a conversation between an Aladdin solutions engineer and an Alpha FMC consultant on whether asset managers should build or buy their investment technology, framed around three live audience polls. The panel argues that the answer is a hybrid: buy a governed, open "backbone" (an investment book of record with APIs and events) and spend scarce engineering talent on differentiating work at the periphery, with data quality and governance as the precondition for any AI use. 6. **Sewdass on the modular, API-first backbone (2.1, 2.3 or 3.3 Openness).** "I think that that word monolith is definitely being challenged in the market. I don't think that clients are OK with that monolithic approach anymore." (line 132). "I would say the shift that we're seeing and very much accelerating over the last 2024 months is this move to more of a modular approach where clients are looking for that backbone foundational data that supports both publics and privates workflows that are highly governed, but. That backbone to then be able to fully open up and kind of be a mechanism by which clients can build a top of. So from an infrastructure perspective, that requires, you know, very, very robust APIs that manage the end to end life cycle of a transaction. So if clients want to build, you know, their secret sauce rebalancing tools or defined optimizers, they can absolutely do so. (lines 132-134; "2024 months" is almost certainly "24 months"; "slain clean" is caption noise for "same clean"). Also: "whereas they can choose to live in the tools exclusively or they can choose to pull things out, push things in in real time and still be able to build on top of it" (line 136).

7. **Sewdass on the hybrid pitch, IBOR as backbone (3.3 Whole portfolio / Openness).** "Increasingly what we're seeing is that you don't have to choose one or the other. So maintaining that competitive edge while eliminating some of the costs associated with infrastructure integration that you're able to leverage, you know, an investment book of record where you're able to leverage that backbone infrastructure, which is foundational data, which is foundational workflows that are integrated across portfolio management, trading operations. And then build on top of that knowing that you have the core infrastructure and governance that's aligning all of these core workflows." (line 36). Five-year prediction: "It's not a decision on going all in on building. It's not a decision on buying. It's that hybrid approach. You'll need that common infrastructure." (line 162).

8. **Sewdass on public and private markets in one data model (3.3 Public and private together).** "there's really two paradigms that private markets fall into. Either they started off as a public investment shop and they have some point solutions that are off to the side ... in many cases what we see, it's fitting a circle into a square." (line 64). "Or does it logically make more sense to utilise a data structure and a transactional structure that natively supports both publics and privates wholesale?" (line 70). On private-market data: "it's not a simple buy sell, it's more event based in nature. You have, you have fundings, you have commitments, you have distributions, you have all of these different capital calls ... everything from identifiers are not standardised." (line 66).

9. **Sewdass on "system of record" plus "system of work" and on AI needing governed data (1.8 or 3.3 AI).** "you need that system of record that underpins anything that AI is built upon. So integrated work flows across portfolio management, trading, operations, compliance, all of that, you know, highly governed with the appropriate operational controls in place" (lines 110-112). Client anecdote: "We had ACIO trying to get an answer on what is my exposure to oil and what is my exposure to chemicals that might be impacted by the blockage on the straight of. ... the response that came back in three different prompts was three different answers" (line 100); "you need AI as an enabler.
