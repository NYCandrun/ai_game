4. **eFront integration into a whole-portfolio view, 2024 (1.4 eFront row; 3.3 "Public and private together").** Fitzgibbon, line 48: "especially as we've seen private market investments grow so rapidly, investment portfolios have become more diverse and it just has really enforced the need for having that consolidated view of risk and exposures across both public and private asset classes. And a big challenge there is being able to source the data, managing disparate data sources with different frequencies of reporting and even different styles of calculation ... So yes, you know, Joah mentioned the E-front acquisition within a Latin. We work really hard on bringing to life an integrated solution that can provide insights to the whole portfolio." A third-party view from the Barings CIO, line 42: "Blackrock is working a lot with the recent acquisition on e-front. They are integrating a Latin, traditional Latin risk in e-front, which is public and private into one suite of products." Fitzgibbon on private-market risk models, line 126: "making sure that we're taking a customized and specific approach to different private market assets and then doing our best to bring that all together and, you know, whole portfolio view type solutions so that our users, our clients are having to go into, you know, different systems in order to have a consolidated view of their portfolio." (The "are having to" reads as a caption drop of "aren't having to".)

5. **iBOR and ABOR as the single source of truth for risk, performance and client reporting (1.1, beside Khara and Newman on IBOR).** Fitzgibbon, line 74: "A big part of that is having that central source of truth. So when everything's focused in one system around a portfolio and you can rely on the iBOR, the A-BOR to really be the source of truth and the risk analytics and the performance and the portfolio manager and the risk team and the client are all receiving the analytics and the reports out of the same system." Line 76: "as much as we can really center our workflows around a system of record, I think that goes a long way in our ability to quickly react to client questions and keep data consistency across the firm front and center."

6. **Exception-based monitoring in Aladdin, stated in 2024 (1.6, beside Fitzgibbon's July 2026 line and the Risk Radar / RiskException description).** Fitzgibbon, line 78: "A major transformation that I've seen occur in the last couple of years and particularly within Aladdin is the shift toward exception based monitoring. ... there's a lot of workflows behind the scenes that are running overnight on analytics and on portfolios or certain security exposures or whatever it is so that when we come to work in the morning, rather than looking at this large landscape of data and looking for those outliers, instead it's really been isolated to the point that a risk manager or portfolio manager can quickly react to something that may or may not be something that requires attention." Line 80: "Is this something that I need to address today? Is this something that I want to maybe monitor for a couple of days and then come back to you? Do I need to have a conversation about it? And then being able to track all of that in a technology solution is just really powerful for firms."

7. **Risk oversight at scale, including too little risk (1.6).** Fitzgibbon, line 50: "we also want to enable our users, our portfolio managers, to understand if they're taking on too little risk or not the right risks." Line 52: "a Latin can really help our users to understand and monitor truly at scale whether portfolios are behaving as they're expected to and in line with the investment objectives." Line 54: "we see scale as being able to provide, you know, a systematised approach to risk oversight ... being able to have a really broad breadth of products, whether that be applications or data or analytics, and then being able to just adapt with the market is super crucial."

8. **"Change alpha" in use in 2024 (3.3 "Direction"; 3.2 Pia Schaefer row, which dates it to T2-011 Jul 2025 and T2-012 Apr 2024).** Fitzgibbon, line 102: "within a lot of, and we recently started talking about this concept of change alpha and that's understanding is your firm position to be agile enough to seek advantage of opportunity and navigate the CF change. So I think it's the ability to navigate with agility." ("CF change" is probably "sea of change".)

9. **Data quality and unstructured data (1.7).** Fitzgibbon, line 84: "having more centralized software solutions that reduce the data redundancy and then the ability to wrangle data in a way where it's possible to understand the incoming data and set up guardrails around what that is with alerts so that people understand if one system isn't contributing in the way that it needs to be." Line 86: "being able to handle unstructured data sets in a way that's scalable has also become increasingly important."

10. **Data governance for AI (1.8 or 3.8).** Fitzgibbon, line 116: "Data governance is really no longer like just like nice to have or an actor thought when we're training gen AI models or using any of these AI tools. We really have to make sure that we can determine the lineage of the data source, make sure the data is accurate, you know, and just ensure that you have the right entitlements and controls around the data in place." ("an actor thought" is "an afterthought".) She does not mention Aladdin Copilot in this recording.

11. **Panellist firms' own stacks (3.5 row for Franklin Templeton and Barings; 3.9).** Kenny, line 64: "those are all different modules of a proprietary platform that we have within Franklin Templeton investment solutions called Mosaic"; line 112: "we're developing a generative AI tool to help with the usage of this internal platform ... also as we start to roll out to clients as well". Malik, line 90: "in order for to take advantage of co-pilot and Microsoft Office 365, you know, your data, you unstructured data needs to be organized and understood as well"; line 92: "We have gone through media transformation and bearings recently."
