2. **BlackRock's data-lifecycle framework (belongs in 1.7).** Skinner, lines 50-52: "when you think about the challenges, we sort of break it down into the data lifecycle, right? So if you're an organization who's managing a series of portfolios, you need to first source data. So determining which data you're gonna use, where you're gonna get it from, what sources provide the most fidelity and accuracy, you then have to ingest all that data and normalize it, right? ... So how do you think about centralization of that data once you bring it in? You need to think about a governance framework that's gonna sit over top of all of that. So how are you gonna monitor when the data feeds are coming in? What's the quality of that data who has access and permissions to that data across the organization? And then finally, that's when you get to the exciting stuff where you can start to analyze the data and actually derive insights and investment decisions off of it."

3. **What Aladdin does with data quality (belongs in 1.7).** Skinner, line 92: "there's a huge amount of work that we do when we think about kind of the data picture here at Aladdin where we do a lot of the checking, processing, pre-processing of information, but then also maintain the governance framework for checking to make sure there's accuracy in that data, that the data sources that we're getting information from have timely delivery of data due to downstream and, you know, a dependent processes."

4. **Why BlackRock bought eFront: pension clients, 100 to 200 funds, a managed data service (belongs in 1.2 "Private markets" and the eFront row of 1.4).** Skinner, line 68: "we saw this problem coming up across client portfolios, it stretches back over 10 years ago at this point. And I used to actually cover a series of our pension clients who are using the Aladdin platform here. And they had this problem, you know, in spades, given the low rate environment, they were pushing into higher yielding assets that drove them into privates into private equity real estate infrastructure, private credit more recently. And they didn't have a great solution for sourcing data from the 100, 200 funds that they were LP investors in. So we made an acquisition in 2019 of a firm called E-Front and one of the, one of the nice things that we really liked about them was they had a managed data service." Line 70: "So they could go out and source this information from the GPs individually, collect it, bring it back in and at least normalize it within the case of thinking about multi-alternative aspects." The document's eFront row (1.4) does not mention a managed data service or this rationale.

How do you align public and private markets? Think about data model, think about transaction model, think about risk factor framework and the calibration of those models. Think about how you're going to build a parsimonious performance based model, how you're going to choose, like which methodology for converting to time-weighted return you're going to use, how you're going to adjust for certain cash flows that are different in the private markets versus publics." Line 64, on the adjustments: "The point on, you know, essentially taking quarterly data, de-smoothing it in like in certain industries as well too. If we get away from private equity, you also have appraisal based valuations, for instance, in the real estate market, there are all sorts of different adjustments and transformations that need to be made to the data".

And how do you track all these things and their impact in the portfolio?"

7. **Commoditised versus differentiated data, what "your platform" should do (belongs in 1.7 or beside the Build vs Buy closing line in 3.6).** Skinner, lines 108-110: "very few, if any investors out there are generating alpha off of, having the best security master data or pricing data ... And so we see people thinking more strategically about this where they are going to outsource the commoditized data functions within their investment organization and double down and focus on the differentiated datasets. So you'll have your platform do the security master price index data, sectoring data, ratings information, maybe ESG data and then you're gonna spend it on that, alpha generating dataset, creating signals or whatever it may be that gives you your investment edge overall." Line 66, on public data vendors: "With public markets at least, there are a myriad of data providers on the street that will sell you the data and it's easy, pretty easy to get, you know, security master, index data, pricing information, sectoring and for ESG info ... In the private markets, given you are the, you know, the investor is the asset owner themselves, they are the ones who is often having to collect the data".
